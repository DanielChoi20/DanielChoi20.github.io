Operations Management · Sustainable Systems

Donghyun (Daniel) Choi

Ph.D. Candidate in Operations Management
Georgia Tech Scheller College of Business

I study how firms manage the transition to a sustainable economy and how policy and system design can make low-carbon and circular operations both resilient and economically viable. I am advised by André Calmon and Beril Toktay, and I combine empirical methods and analytical modeling to study corporate climate commitments, transition risk, recycled-content policy, and circular supply chains. Before Georgia Tech, I was a researcher at KAIST's Center for Sustainable Development and an instructor officer at the Korea Army Academy. I aim to identify research questions from practice, answer them rigorously, and return useful insights to firms and policymakers.

Research

From ambitious targets to workable systems.

My work spans two connected streams: managing climate risk through science-based targets and designing economically viable systems for material circularity.

01

Corporate climate strategy

Why firms commit to externally validated targets—and how markets interpret those commitments.

Under review at Production and Operations Management

Slack and Science-Based Target Commitments

Donghyun Choi, Abhinav Shubham, and Manpreet Hora

Using a global panel of 13,465 firms from 2014–2021, we show that liquid resources and resources embedded in organizational routines have opposite associations with climate-target commitment.

Public article ↗
Work in progress

Coupled by Commitment? Science-Based Targets and Market-Perceived Climate Risk

Abhinav Shubham, I. Attari, and Donghyun Choi

We study whether committing to a science-based target changes perceived climate-risk exposure and whether that signal depends on peer commitments.

02

Material circularity

How regulation, allocation rules, market competition, and technology shape circular supply chains.

Preparing for submission to Management Science

Closing the Loop or Opening the Market? Implications of Recycled Content Mandate and Allocation Policy

Donghyun Choi, André Calmon, and L. Beril Toktay

We examine how recycled-content mandates and priority access to recycled material affect circularity, procurement, and the financial stability of bottle-collection systems.

Novelis Graduate Scholar ↗
Work in progress

Ordered-Quality Process Flexibility in Recycling Networks

Donghyun Choi, Chen Tang, and André Calmon

We ask when advanced recycling technologies create system value in networks where material streams differ in quality and flexibility is one-directional.
