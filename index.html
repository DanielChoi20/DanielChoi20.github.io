Hi! I am Donghyun (Daniel) Choi, PhD Candidate in Operations Management at Georgia Institute of Technology, advised by André Calmon and Beril Toktay. My research investigates the operational implications of sustainability targets and provides managerial and policy insights using analytical modeling and empirical methods. I was nominated as a Novelis Graduate Scholar and am closely working with companies in the Drawdown Georgia Business Compact. I am on the 2026–2027 academic job market.

Research

My research studies how firms commit resources when they face sustainability targets, whether those targets are imposed by policy or set voluntarily. The first stream examines mandated targets in packaging supply chains. Under packaging extended producer responsibility (EPR), producers must meet collection targets, recycling targets, and recycled-content mandates. These rules turn recycled material into a second supply source with its own price, quality, and availability constraints. The second stream examines voluntary targets. Firms face growing pressure to reduce emissions across their operations and supply chains, and many respond by committing to the Science Based Targets initiative (SBTi). I study which internal resource positions make firms more likely to commit, and how equity markets reassess firms’ climate risk after they commit. Prior to my PhD studies, I also conducted research on developing resilient energy systems.

Closing the Loop or Opening the Market? Implications of Recycled Content Mandate and Allocation Policy

Donghyun Choi, André Calmon, and L. Beril Toktay

We examine how recycled-content mandates and priority access to recycled material affect circularity, procurement, and the financial stability of bottle-collection systems.

Donghyun Choi, Chen Tang, and André Calmon

We ask when advanced recycling technologies create system value in networks where material streams differ in quality and flexibility is one-directional.

Slack and Science-Based Target Commitments

Donghyun Choi, Abhinav Shubham, and Manpreet Hora

Using a global panel of 13,465 firms from 2014–2021, we show that liquid resources and resources embedded in organizational routines have opposite associations with climate-target commitment.
