Research

Building credible transitions and workable circular systems.

I combine empirical methods and analytical modeling to study how firms respond to sustainability targets and how policies and technologies can improve circular supply chains.

01

Corporate climate strategy

Managing climate risk through science-based targets

I study why firms commit to externally validated decarbonization targets and how investors interpret those commitments. This work examines organizational resources, supply-chain emissions, and changing market perceptions of climate risk.

Under review at POMEmpirical

Slack and Science-Based Target Commitments

with Anish Shubham and Manpreet Hora

Why do some firms commit to science-based climate targets while others wait? Using a global panel of 13,465 firms from 2014–2021, we show that liquid resources and resources embedded in organizational routines have opposite associations with commitment.

Discrete-time hazard model · SBTi, CDP, and financial records

Work in progressEmpirical

Coupled by Commitment? Science-Based Targets and Market-Perceived Climate Risk

with Anish Shubham and I. Attari

We study whether committing to a science-based target changes how investors perceive a firm's exposure to climate risk—and whether that signal depends on peer commitments.

Climate beta · Stacked difference-in-differences

I study how regulation, allocation rules, market competition, and recycling technologies shape both circularity and the economics of collection systems.

Looking ahead

Voluntary infrastructure for circularity

My near-term research investigates how voluntary coalitions can build financially self-sustaining recycling infrastructure where mandates and subsidies are absent. Georgia's emerging voluntary extended-producer-responsibility efforts provide a motivating setting for studying coalition structure and funding mechanisms.
